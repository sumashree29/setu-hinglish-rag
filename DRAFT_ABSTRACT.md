# Draft Abstract

Code-mixed languages, such as Hinglish (Hindi-English), pose unique challenges for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems due to extreme lexical variance, varying entity densities, and complex semantic structures. Recent architectures have proposed mitigating these issues via complex corrective pipelines—utilizing operations such as latent projection, entity preservation, and translation—orchestrated by reinforcement learning controllers. In this paper, we conduct a rigorous, leakage-free empirical audit of such a context-adaptive pipeline (SETU) against strong zero-shot dense multilingual baselines (BGE-M3, mE5-large). Through strict Out-Of-Fold cross-validation and multiple-testing correction, we demonstrate that state-of-the-art dense models intrinsically resolve up to 80% of Hinglish queries zero-shot. Furthermore, we uncover a systemic "over-correction" phenomenon: applying explicit corrective operators statistically degrades the performance of initially successful retrievals while failing to provide robust corrective lift on actual failure cases. Finally, we show that without carefully balanced context features, adaptive controllers collapse into static early-termination policies. Our findings indicate that rather than engineering brittle, dataset-specific operator pipelines, scaling generic multilingual representation models offers a more robust path forward for code-mixed RAG.
