# Draft Abstract

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems critically depend on candidate retrieval: if passage retrieval fails, downstream generation suffers error propagation. In South Asia, hundreds of millions of users formulate search queries in Hinglish—Hindi phonetically transcribed into Latin script and blended with English vocabulary. To mitigate code-mixed failure, conventional pipelines deploy corrective operators spanning latent projection (LQP), entity normalization (CAEP), and adaptive gating (LAG). In this paper, we conduct a leakage-free (5-fold GroupKFold) empirical audit of such pipelines across dense multilingual bi-encoders (BGE-M3, mE5-large, Indic-SBERT, mContriever) and BM25 on 314 banking/policy Hinglish queries and 500 out-of-domain Dolly-Hinglish QA pairs. We show that web-scale dense encoders natively resolve 77.4–80.9% of in-domain and 83.2–88.6% of external Hinglish queries zero-shot without query modification. Crucially, we uncover a systemic overcorrection hazard: unconditionally applying standalone corrective operators statistically degrades initially successful retrievals (Delta MRR between -0.0310 and -0.0757 in-domain, and -0.0182 to -0.0874 out-of-domain, Holm-corrected p <= 0.0220) while failing to deliver statistically robust lift from standalone operators on baseline failures in-domain (p >= 0.3300). Under grouped OOF evaluation, a learned LinUCB selective router (SETU_v2) avoids significant overcorrection (Delta MRR = -0.0075, p = 0.9225) and significantly recovers failed queries (Delta MRR = +0.0364, p = 0.0494) while reducing mean actions by 54.25% (3.00 to 1.37) and latency by 59.1% (1186.8 ms to 485.1 ms), despite repeated-action guard terminations (75.8%). Finally, comparing selective routing policies against an empirical oracle (MRR = 0.8842, leaving 89.5% of queries unmodified) and confirming strong score-margin confidence correlation (rho = 0.5030, p = 1.07e-20), we establish grounded design guidelines for code-mixed enterprise RAG.
